# Pepperdine Supplement: Faith Essay

**Prompt:** Pepperdine is a Christian university where all are welcomed and encouraged to challenge each other in the pursuit of truth. Students, faculty, and staff members from all backgrounds participate in conversations of faith inside and outside of the classroom. Considering that Pepperdine is a Christian university, why are you interested in attending and how would you contribute to conversations of faith on campus?

**Draft (490 words). The two bracketed parts are made-up examples. Replace them with true details from your life.**

I'm an atheist, so when I first saw that Pepperdine was a Christian school, I almost scrolled past it. I figured I'd either feel out of place or spend four years being told what to believe. Then I came across something George Pepperdine said about the Christian way of life: "We do not compel you to accept it. You are free to make your own choice, but we want you to know what it is." That changed things for me. No one would force me to believe anything, but I'd get a real chance to understand something that matters to a lot of people I love.

[Most of my family is Christian. Every Thanksgiving my grandma has us all hold hands for grace, and I never know if I'm supposed to close my eyes.] We're close, but we almost never talk about faith in a real way. Someone always changes the subject before it gets awkward. It feels like we're skipping the most interesting conversations we could have, like why they believe what they do, or how I figure out right and wrong without religion. I want to go to a school where people actually talk about this stuff.

One line from Pepperdine's Affirmation Statement stuck with me, the idea that truth has "nothing to fear from investigation." I agree with that completely, and I think it applies to my own beliefs too. I've never had to explain my atheism to someone who knows a lot about Christianity. If I'm going to keep believing what I believe, I should be able to do that around smart people who disagree with me. And if I can't, that would tell me something.

I think what I'd bring is an honest outside perspective. I'm not interested in arguing anyone out of their faith. I'd take Chapel and my religion classes seriously, and I'm curious to read the Bible with people who know it well, since it's shaped so many people in my life. I'd ask the questions I really want answered and then actually listen. It would probably be good for my Christian classmates to talk with someone who sees things differently, the same way it would be good for me. I'd also want other non-religious students to see they don't have to sit these conversations out.

I also think we'd agree on more than people expect. Pepperdine's motto is "Freely ye received, freely give." I don't believe in God, but I do believe in that. [I've volunteered at our local food bank for two years, and some of my best conversations about faith have happened there, mostly while sorting canned goods.] I'd want to keep doing that kind of service at Pepperdine, next to people whose reasons are different from mine.

I don't know exactly how four years at Pepperdine would change me. I just know I'd come out understanding faith a lot better than I do now, and probably myself too.
